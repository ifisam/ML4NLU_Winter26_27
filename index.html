ML4NLU: Machine Learning for Natural Language Understanding

Winter 26/27 Semester

Prof. Dr. Achim Rettinger Chair of Computational Linguistics · Universität Trier

Overview

ML4NLU examines how natural language can be understood by machines — starting from the mathematical and machine-learning foundations, through neural classifiers, static and contextual word representations, sequence models and transformers, pretraining and post-training, up to prompting, tool-augmented and multi-lingual/multi-modal/multi-agent LLMs and the ethical questions they raise. The course runs as a semi-flipped classroom: each week pairs an asynchronous lecture video with an in-class discussion, a student seminar presentation, and a hands-on tutorial.

Challenge Bonus

An optional challenge runs alongside the written report. Your challenge result adjusts your written-report grade:

  • Beat our baseline → +1 grading step
  • Hand in the best-performing model → +2 grading steps

Participation is optional and does not replace the written report or exam.

FAQ

How is my final grade calculated?
50% written report (schriftliche Ausarbeitung), 50% written exam. The exam mixes flipped-classroom-style questions with applied questions from the tutorial/exercise sheets.
When is the written report due?
Officially end of February; the hard deadline is end of March (end of semester) as listed in PORTA. Missing the hard deadline counts as a failed attempt and requires re-examination next semester.
Is attendance mandatory?
Lecture and tutorial attendance is voluntary (hybrid); seminar attendance is compulsory with active participation.
What does a typical week look like?
Tue night — new lecture video + flipped-classroom questions posted. Thu lecture — discussion (≤30 min) + summary presentation (≤60 min, streamed). Thu/Fri seminar — student group presentations. Following Mon tutorial — exercises, Q&A, hands-on practice.
Where do the lecture videos come from?
Curated primarily from Stanford's CS224U (NLU) and CS224N (NLP with Deep Learning) YouTube playlists, plus Meta's LLaMA technical reports for later topics.
Who do I contact?
Technical issues with Porta → porta@uni-trier.de. Student records / login credentials → admission@uni-trier.de. Course-specific questions go through StudIP messaging.
Do I need to register for exams myself?
Yes — you must sign up for both the written exam and the written report individually.
